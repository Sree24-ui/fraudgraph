EXPLAINABLE RULES · V1

What a signal means

The detector evaluates trailing 5-minute, 30-minute, and 2-hour event-time windows. It requires at least four distinct sources, then four distinct recipients, a 65–135% ratio of payouts to observed inflow, and at least 80% of recipients absent from the source set.

Amounts use integer paise. These are engineering thresholds, not reporting thresholds or legal definitions. The score is a heuristic; it is not a calibrated likelihood of fraud.

Known blind spots

  • Coordinated hubs with fewer than four sources or recipients can evade detection.
  • Slow redistribution beyond two hours, partial payouts, recipient reuse, and interleaved draining can evade detection.
  • Legitimate aggregators can look identical and produce false positives.
  • Equal timestamps cannot establish payment order. The detector does not invent an order.

Independent evaluation failed to generalize

The unchanged rule produced zero alerts on 885,744 transactions from IBM AMLSim bank_mixed: 0 of 753 SAR-labelled accounts and 0 of 50 gather-scatter hubs were detected. Precision is undefined because there were no alerts. This external dataset is also synthetic, has day-level timestamps, and its gather-scatter patterns span 9–29 days. This is not evidence of UPI accuracy.

Operating boundary

This build is a single-node investigation service. It has not been validated on real UPI traffic or national-network scale. A real deployment needs institution-specific calibration, identity and transaction-source integration, TLS, protected backups, monitoring, retention controls, and independent security review.
